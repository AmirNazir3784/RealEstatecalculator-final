Understanding Real Estate Commission

A sale price is not the same as an agent's earnings. This calculator first finds the total commission, takes referral and franchise fees off the top, splits the remainder between the listing and buyer sides, then applies your share and subtracts the transaction fee you enter.

The terms behind the estimate

Sale price
The property price used as the calculation base.
Commission rate
The percentage of that price used to calculate the stated gross commission.
Agent split
Your percentage of the applicable side after referral and franchise fees.
Listing share
The percentage of the total commission that goes to the listing side; the buyer side gets the rest.
Referral and franchise fees
Percentages taken off the total commission before it is split between the sides.
Net to seller
Sale price minus the full total commission, other closing costs and any mortgage payoff.
Transaction fee
The fixed amount deducted from your share in this model.

Worked example

Worked example

From sale price to your share

Inputs

Choose Agent Earnings and keep your role set to Both sides, with referral and franchise fees at 0%. Choose Agent Earnings and keep your role set to Both sides, with referral and franchise fees at 0%. Use a $450,000 sale price, a 3% commission rate, a 70% agent split and a $295 transaction fee.

Calculation
  1. Gross commission: $450,000 × 0.03$13,500.00
  2. Your share: $13,500 × 0.70$9,450.00
  3. ResultAfter fee: $9,450 − $295$9,155.00
Explanation

The final amount is before taxes and any costs not entered.

What changes the result?

A higher sale price, rate or agent split increases your share when the other inputs stay the same. A higher fee reduces it dollar for dollar. Rates and splits vary; the example is not a standard or prescribed arrangement.

What is not included?

The model does not deduct taxes, referral payments beyond the percentage you enter, team splits, broker caps or other business costs. A fee larger than your share can produce a negative result.

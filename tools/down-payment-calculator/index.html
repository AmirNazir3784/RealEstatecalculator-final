The Short Version

Down Payment Calculator: The Quick Answer

Use this down payment calculator to estimate your upfront home buying costs, mortgage loan amount, monthly principal and interest, and private mortgage insurance (PMI). Enter your home price, down payment, interest rate, and loan term to see how much you’ll need to buy a home.

Example: On a $400,000 home, a 10% down payment is $40,000. With 3% closing costs, your estimated cash to close is $52,000.

How to use: Enter the home price, set your down payment as dollars or as a percentage, and add your expected rate and term. Open Adjust Closing Costs & PMI to change the closing-cost percentage, override the PMI estimate, or subtract earnest money you have already paid. Select any card in the tier comparison to load that tier into the calculator.

The Formula

How a Down Payment Calculator Works: The Formula, Step by Step

A down payment calculator estimates how much you need upfront and how much you’ll borrow based on your home price and down payment percentage.

Down paymentHome price × Down payment percentage
Loan amountHome price − Down payment
Loan-to-value (LTV)Loan amount ÷ Home price × 100
Monthly PMILoan amount × Annual PMI rate ÷ 12
Cash to closeDown payment + Closing costs − Earnest money

Your estimated monthly principal and interest are calculated using your loan amount, interest rate, and mortgage term.

Minimums

How Much Down Payment Do You Need? Minimums by Loan Type

You need as little as 0–3.5% under the main loan programs — the minimum depends on the loan type and, for some programs, your credit score and income. Lenders can require more than the program minimum. For a first-time homebuyer, the down payment is often far smaller than the 20% figure most people carry in their heads.

Down Payment Percentages Compared: 3%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20%

On a $400,000 home, the down payment alone is: 3% = $12,000 · 3.5% (FHA) = $14,000 · 5% = $20,000 · 10% = $40,000 · 15% = $60,000 · 20% = $80,000 — before closing costs. Smaller tiers get you in sooner but borrow more and add mortgage insurance; larger tiers cost more on day one and less every month after. The tier cards above price all of it — upfront cash, monthly payment, and PMI — on your own price and rate, which is the only comparison that matters.

Choosing a Loan

Which Loan Type Fits Your Down Payment?

Not all mortgages are created equal, and the right one depends on your credit score, your service history, where the home is, and how much cash you want to keep in the bank. Different loans come with different rules for down payments, interest rates and mortgage insurance:

  • Conventional fits buyers with stronger credit who want insurance they can cancel — PMI ends at 80% LTV, and 3% down is possible for eligible first-time buyers.
  • FHA fits buyers whose credit score shuts the conventional door — 3.5% down at 580+, in exchange for mortgage insurance that can last the life of the loan.
  • VA fits eligible veterans, service members and surviving spouses — 0% down and no monthly insurance at all, with a one-time funding fee instead.
  • USDA fits buyers in eligible rural areas within the income limits — 0% down with the lowest annual insurance fee of the four.

Pick the loan type first and the down payment percentage second: the program decides which tiers are even open to you.

How Your Loan Type Changes the Total Cost

Your loan type changes more than the cash you need on day one — it sets the cost baseline for the entire time you hold the loan. A smaller down payment gets you through the door faster, but it borrows more and adds insurance; a larger one costs more upfront and less every month after. On the same $400,000 home at 6.5% over 30 years, this calculator prices the trade-off:

RouteDown paymentCash to closeMonthly P&I + insurance
Conventional, 3% down$12,000$24,000$2,760 (incl. PMI)
FHA, 3.5% down$14,000$26,000$2,659 (incl. MIP)

Two lessons hide in those rows. First, FHA’s monthly figure can beat conventional at similar down payments — but its insurance may never switch off, while conventional PMI ends at 80% LTV. Second, the 20% route costs $68,000 more on day one than the 3% route and saves $737 a month — whether that trade is worth it depends on how long you keep the loan. The 20% Question

Do You Need a 20% Down Payment on a House?

No. You don’t need a 20% down payment to buy a home. Eligible borrowers can qualify for conventional mortgages with much smaller down payments.

However, conventional loans generally require Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) when your down payment is below 20%.

PMI increases your monthly mortgage payment but can eventually be removed:

  • 80% LTV: You can generally request PMI cancellation when your loan balance reaches 80% of the home’s original value, subject to eligibility requirements.
  • 78% LTV: PMI generally ends automatically when the scheduled loan balance reaches 78%, provided you’re current on payments.

FHA mortgage insurance (MIP) follows different rules. Depending on your down payment, it may remain for the entire loan term.

Mortgage Insurance

What Is PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance), and When Does It End?

PMI typically costs about 0.2%–1.5% of the loan per year depending on credit score and down payment. Under Consumer Financial Protection Bureau rules, you can request cancellation when your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and PMI must terminate automatically at 78% of the original value if you are current on payments.

Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender, not you, if you stop paying. On a conventional loan you generally pay it when you put down less than 20%.

Three things buyers routinely miss:

  1. The monthly figure hides the total. At this calculator's $180/month estimate, PMI held for the 7–9 years a 10%-down loan can take to reach 78% LTV adds up to more than $15,000. Judge PMI by its lifetime cost, not its monthly cost.
  2. Extra principal ends it sooner. Paying down to 80% LTV faster — then requesting cancellation in writing — cuts years of PMI.
  3. FHA is different. FHA loans charge MIP instead of PMI: with less than 10% down, MIP lasts the life of the loan, and refinancing into a conventional loan is the usual way out.

This calculator prices PMI into every tier below 20% using the rate in the Adjust Closing Costs & PMI panel (0.60% per year by default). It is an estimate — real PMI is tiered by credit score and LTV, and your lender's quote replaces it.

Total Cash

Cash to Close: How Much Cash You Need Beyond the Down Payment

Your down payment isn’t the only upfront expense when buying a home. Cash to close is the amount you need to complete the purchase.

It can include:

  • Closing costs: Lender fees, appraisal, title services, and recording fees.
  • Prepaid expenses: Homeowners insurance, property taxes, and prepaid mortgage interest.
  • Earnest money: Your initial deposit, credited toward the amount due at closing.
  • Credits and assistance: Eligible seller credits, lender credits, gift funds, and down payment assistance.

Closing costs commonly range from 2% to 5% of the home’s purchase price, depending on the location and transaction.

Remember that the calculator’s estimate may not include every prepaid expense or adjustment. Review your lender’s closing disclosure for the actual amount due.

Saving Up

How Long Does It Take to Save a Down Payment?

Your savings timeline depends on your target cash to close, current savings, and monthly contributions.

Formula:

Months to Save(Target Cash to Close − Current Savings) ÷ Monthly Savings

Keep money aside for emergencies, moving expenses, and unexpected home repairs instead of using all your savings at closing.

Avoid These

5 Down Payment Mistakes That Cost Buyers Money

The same five, repeatedly:

  1. Budgeting for the down payment only — then discovering closing costs and prepaids at the Loan Estimate stage.
  2. Chasing 20% automatically — spending years saving to avoid a PMI that might have cost less than the rent paid while waiting.
  3. Draining every account to close — leaving no reserve for the repairs, furnishings and surprises of the first year.
  4. Ignoring gift-fund paperwork — large undocumented deposits and missing gift letters are a common, avoidable cause of underwriting delays.
  5. Comparing loans on the down payment alone — FHA's lower down payment can cost more over time than conventional with PMI, because FHA MIP can last the life of the loan. Compare total cash, monthly payment, and insurance duration together.

Fine Print, Up Front

Down Payment Calculator Assumptions and Limitations

  • Results are estimates from your inputs, for planning only — not a loan quote, an offer, or financial advice. For decisions this size, a lender and, where tax or legal questions arise, a qualified professional are worth consulting.
  • Closing costs are estimated as one percentage of the home price (default 3%; typical range 2–5%). Actual costs vary by state, lender and loan type.
  • PMI is estimated from the single annual rate shown in the calculator; real pricing is tiered by credit score and LTV.
  • FHA figures follow HUD's published premiums; VA monthly figures exclude the funding fee, which is normally financed into the loan.
  • Monthly figures here are principal, interest and estimated PMI only. Taxes, homeowners insurance and HOA dues are in the Mortgage Calculator.
  • Program minimums and insurance rules are those published by the CFPB, HUD and the VA as of November 2026; lenders may impose stricter requirements.

Remember

Key Takeaways

  • Your real target is cash to close — down payment plus closing costs — not the down payment alone.
  • Minimum down payments are 0% (VA/USDA), 3% (conventional, with conditions), and 3.5% (FHA). Twenty percent is optional.
  • On a $400,000 home: 10% down = $40,000, and about $52,000 total cash at 3% closing costs.
  • PMI ends by request at 80% LTV and automatically at 78% LTV on conventional loans; judge it by its lifetime cost, not the monthly figure.
  • FHA MIP with under 10% down lasts the life of the loan — a key difference from cancellable PMI.
  • Save toward a tier that still leaves you a 3–6 month emergency reserve after closing.
  • Compare tiers on total cash + monthly payment + insurance duration together — never on the down payment alone.

Bottom Line

Down Payment Calculator: The Bottom Line

A down payment is not a single number — it is a choice between tiers, each with its own upfront cash, monthly payment, and mortgage-insurance cost. The buyers who get it right plan for the total cash to close, pick the tier that protects their emergency fund, and know exactly when their PMI ends.
